Part 1 — Warm-up: quick-fire questions

Answer each question out loud in 2–3 sentences. Use the conditional in the question in your answer. Try not to pause for more than a few seconds.

  1. What happens if you miss a filing deadline in the US? (zero)
  2. What will you do if you have a free weekend after your exams? (first)
  3. If you could live in any city in California, which one would you choose? (second)
  4. If you hadn't studied law, what would you have studied? (third)
  5. What do you usually do if a client is angry or upset? (zero)
  6. If you could have dinner with any judge or lawyer in history, who would it be? (second)
speaking time
• Does that rule work the same way in Brazil? What happens there if…?
• And if that plan falls through, what will you do instead?
• Why that city and not somewhere else? What would you miss about where you live now?
• Do you think you would have been happier in that career? Why (not)?

Part 2 — Legal dilemmas: what would you do?

Read each situation. Explain what you would do and why, and what might happen as a result. Use the second conditional and at least one alternative such as unless, as long as, otherwise.

A. A long-standing client asks you to backdate a document "just by a few days — it's only a formality." He is your firm's biggest account.
B. You find out that a classmate in your LL.M. program has copied large parts of a published article in a paper that is worth 40% of the final grade.
C. A big firm in New York offers you a job with double your salary, but you would have to leave California before finishing your master's.
D. During a negotiation, the other side accidentally sends you an internal email that reveals their lowest acceptable offer.
speaking time
Useful language: If I were in that position, I'd… · I wouldn't… unless… · I'd probably… otherwise… · The risk would be that… · To be honest, if it were up to me…
• Would your answer change if the client weren't so important to the firm?
• What would happen if you did nothing at all?
• Would a lawyer in Brazil be expected to act the same way?
• Is there a situation where you would accept the offer / use the information?

Part 3 — Turning points: looking back

Talk about the past using the third conditional. Then connect it to the present with a mixed conditional (If I hadn't…, I wouldn't be… now).

  1. Describe the decision to leave Brazil and work in the US. If you hadn't made that decision, how would your career have been different?
  2. Think of a case, exam or project that didn't go as planned. What would have happened if you had done something differently?
  3. Talk about a person (a professor, mentor or colleague) who influenced you. Where would you be now if you hadn't met them?
speaking time
Useful language: Looking back, if I'd… · I'd never have… if… · Had it not been for…, I wouldn't… · If I hadn't…, I wouldn't be… now · In hindsight, I might have…
• Do you have any regrets about that decision?
• If you could go back, would you do it the same way?
• What advice would you give a Brazilian lawyer thinking about doing the same?

Part 4 — Role-play: advising the client

Your teacher plays the client. Explain the options and their consequences clearly and professionally. Use the zero and first conditional, plus at least one formal structure (Should you…, provided that…).

Client: "My landlord says I have to move out in 15 days because he wants to sell the apartment. I've lived here for six years and I've never missed a payment. What happens if I just stay? And if I fight this, how long will it take?"
Client: "I'm a small business owner and a former employee is threatening to sue me for unpaid overtime. If I pay him something now, will this go away? What happens if it goes to court?"
speaking time
Useful language: If you…, the court will usually… · Generally speaking, if a tenant…, the landlord has to… · Should you decide to…, we'll… · Provided that you…, you should be fine · If we don't…, there's a risk that…
Teacher — push back as the client:
• "But what if I can't afford a lawyer?"
• "What would you do if you were me?"
• "And if I lose, what will happen?"
• "Couldn't I just ignore the letter?"

Part 5 — Discussion: the bigger picture

Give your opinion on each statement. Use at least two different conditionals in each answer and support your view with examples.

  1. "If AI tools can draft contracts in seconds, junior lawyers will soon become unnecessary."
  2. "If law school in the US were free, the profession would be much more diverse."
  3. "If more disputes were solved through mediation, courts wouldn't be so overloaded."
  4. "If Brazil had adopted a common law system, the courts would work more efficiently."
speaking time
Useful language: I'd agree up to a point — if…, then… · That would only be true if… · Unless…, I don't see how… · Assuming that…, it might… · If that had happened years ago,…
• Can you think of a counter-argument? What would someone who disagrees say?
• What would have to change for that to happen?
• How does this affect you personally as a lawyer?
